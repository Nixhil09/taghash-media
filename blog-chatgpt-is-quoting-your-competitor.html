ChatGPT is quoting your competitor. Here's why.

AI assistants don't pick the biggest brand or the best one. They pick the one they can understand, verify and quote. Here's how that works, and what to change.

Nikhil Founder & CEO, Taghash Media
In this article The short version Why AI picks one brand over another Six reasons your competitor gets quoted How to check where you stand What to fix first Questions we get asked Check your AI visibility
The short version
  • When someone asks ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini or Google AI Overviews for a recommendation, the answer is assembled from sources the model can find and trust.
  • Your competitor gets quoted because they're easier to understand, easier to verify and easier to lift a sentence from, not necessarily because they're better.
  • The fix is mostly unglamorous: say what you are in one sentence, publish specifics, answer real questions directly, earn mentions on other sites, and make sure AI crawlers can actually read your pages.

01 / The mechanicsWhy AI picks one brand over another

AI assistants that browse the web work roughly like a very fast researcher. They turn a question into searches, read a handful of pages, and write an answer that reflects what those pages agree on. Assistants that answer from memory lean on what showed up consistently in the text they were trained on.

Either way, the model is looking for the same three things: a clear statement of what a company is, evidence from more than one place that the statement is true, and a passage it can quote without rewriting. If your competitor offers all three and you don't, they get the mention. It's not personal, and it's rarely about budget.

02 / The diagnosisSix reasons your competitor gets quoted

01

They say what they are in one sentence.

Their homepage, About page and LinkedIn all open with the same plain description: who they serve, what they do and where. Yours might open with a tagline. Taglines are great for people and useless for models, which need a sentence they can repeat as fact.

02

Other sites agree with them.

Models trust claims they can corroborate. Your competitor shows up in industry roundups, review sites, podcasts, partner pages and community threads, all describing them the same way. A brand that only talks about itself on its own site is hard for a model to verify.

03

Their pages answer questions directly.

Buyers ask assistants things like “what does a paid social agency cost?” or “X vs Y for a small team.” Your competitor has a page that answers exactly that, in the first paragraph, with a clear heading. Long, clever intros push the answer below the fold, which is where models stop reading.

04

Their content is readable by machines.

Some sites load their content with JavaScript that AI crawlers don't run, or block those crawlers in robots.txt by accident. If the crawler sees an empty page, you don't exist in the answer. Server-rendered text, structured data, a sitemap and an llms.txt file all make you easier to read.

05

They publish specifics.

Numbers, prices or price ranges, locations, named clients and dated results. “We drive growth” gives a model nothing to cite; “cost per acquisition down 38% in one quarter” does. Specific, attributable claims are the sentences that end up in answers.

06

They're consistent everywhere.

Same company name, same category, same one-line description across the website, Google Business Profile, LinkedIn, directories and press. Inconsistency makes a model unsure whether two mentions are the same company, so it quotes the one it's sure about.

03 / The testHow to check where you stand

You don't need a tool to start. Write down 20 to 30 questions your buyers actually ask before they hire someone like you. Mix broad ones (“best agency for X”), comparison ones (“A vs B”) and problem ones (“how do I fix Y”). Then run each in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google, and record four things:

  • Mentioned: does your brand appear at all?
  • Position: first, buried in a list, or a footnote?
  • Accuracy: is what it says about you correct and current?
  • Sources: which pages does it cite, and are any of them yours?

The sources column is the useful one. It tells you exactly which sites the model trusts in your category, which is where you need to be mentioned next. Repeat the test monthly; answers drift as models and sources change.

04 / The planWhat to fix first

Week 1

Audit

Run the prompt test above. Check robots.txt allows AI crawlers, and confirm your key pages render as text without JavaScript.

Week 2

Entity

Write one plain-English sentence that describes your company, and use it everywhere. Add Organization, Service and FAQ structured data, plus an llms.txt file.

Week 3

Answers

Turn your buyers' top ten questions into pages or FAQ entries that answer them in the first two sentences, with specifics.

Week 4

Corroboration

Get mentioned where the model already looks: the roundups, directories, partner pages and communities that showed up in your sources column.

None of this replaces SEO. Rankings still matter, because many assistants search the web before they answer. Think of it as making sure that when they do find you, they understand you well enough to recommend you.

05 / FAQQuestions we get asked

What is generative engine optimization (GEO)?

GEO is the work of getting a brand mentioned, described accurately and cited in answers from AI assistants such as ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google AI Overviews.

Is GEO different from SEO?

They overlap. SEO earns rankings in search results; GEO earns mentions and citations inside AI answers. Both depend on clear content, technical health and authority, so they work best together.

How long does it take to show up in AI answers?

Assistants that search the web live can pick up changes within weeks. Answers that rely on a model's training data change more slowly, as new versions are released. Most brands see movement within one to three months of consistent work.

Can I pay to be recommended by ChatGPT?

Organic recommendations aren't for sale. They're earned through the same signals covered above: clarity, specifics, corroboration and crawlability.

What is llms.txt?

llms.txt is a plain-text file at the root of a website that summarizes what the site is and links to its most important pages, in a format that's easy for language models to read. It's a newer convention and works alongside your sitemap, not instead of it.
